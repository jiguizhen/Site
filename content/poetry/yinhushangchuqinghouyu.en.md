The shimmering waters look loveliest beneath the clear blue sky.


The mist-veiled mountains are equally enchanting in the rain.


If I were to compare West Lake to Xi Shi, the legendary beauty,


She is equally captivating, whether simply adorned or richly dressed.


{{< /poem >}}



## Historical Background


During the Xining era (1068–1077) of the Northern Song dynasty, Su Shi served as an administrative official in Hangzhou. On one occasion, he enjoyed a leisurely gathering with friends over wine at West Lake. Their gathering began under clear skies in the morning and continued into the evening, when rain began to fall.


As the weather shifted from sunshine to rain, Su Shi witnessed the lake and its surrounding mountains transform before his eyes. Moved by the beauty of the landscape in both conditions, he composed this celebrated poem.



## Literary Appreciation



### "The shimmering waters look loveliest beneath the clear blue sky"
Here, Su Shi captures the beauty of West Lake on a sunny day. Sunlight dances across the water, creating a sparkling, ever-changing surface.


The focus of this line is the lake itself—the brilliance and vitality of its waters under the open sky. 



### “The mist-veiled mountains are equally enchanting in the rain”
The scene then shifts from water to mountains. In the rain, distant peaks are softened and partially concealed by a veil of mist, lending the landscape a quiet, ethereal beauty.


Rather than diminishing the scenery, the rain reveals another dimension of West Lake's charm. The focus here is on the mountains and their subtle, atmospheric beauty.



### “If I were to compare West Lake to Xi Shi, the legendary beauty, she is equally captivating, whether simply adorned or richly dressed”
Su Shi makes a graceful comparison between West Lake and Xi Shi, one of the Four Great Beauties of ancient China.


Whether dressed in delicate simplicity or adorned in elaborate finery, Xi Shi remains beautiful. In the same way, West Lake is enchanting in both its rain-washed, understated appearance and its sunlit, radiant splendor.


The light makeup evokes the lake's gentle, misty beauty on a rainy day, while heavy makeup suggests its vivid brilliance under the sunshine.


Through this comparison, Su Shi expresses a profound appreciation of beauty that transcends outward appearances and changing conditions.




## JiGuizhen's Word


The enduring appeal of West Lake does not depend on the weather. Under clear skies, it is beautiful; in the rain, it is equally extraordinary.


Beneath this simple yet profound observation lies a broader outlook on life: a sense of openness, ease, and inner freedom that is not easily disturbed by changing external circumstances.


Just as West Lake retains its beauty whether the skies are bright or overcast, a truly expansive mind can remain composed and appreciative amid life's changing conditions.


In this way, Su Shi's poem is more than a celebration of the natural beauty of West Lake. It also offers a timeless reflection on how we perceive the world—with grace, openness, and a spirit that remains undisturbed by the shifting conditions around us.
